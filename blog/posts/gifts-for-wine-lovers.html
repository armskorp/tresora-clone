A bottle of wine is gone by Sunday. For the friend who really loves wine, the one who hosts and talks about the glass as much as what is in it, the best gift is something that makes every glass feel like an occasion.

What makes a great wine gift

  • It lasts longer than the wine. Glasses, a cheese board, a beautiful box, a story to tell at the table.
  • It fits her style. Bold and dramatic, or light and garden-party.
  • It is ready to give. No wrapping, no last-minute gift bag.

The bold choice: Caravaggio Bacchus

Around 1596, Caravaggio painted Bacchus, the Roman god of wine, holding out a glass toward the viewer as if inviting you to the table. The painting now hangs in the Uffizi in Florence, and it is one of the most famous wine images in art history.

The Caravaggio Bacchus Wine Gift Box turns that invitation into a gift:

  • two thin-stem wine glasses
  • a cable-knit throw in forest green or Bacchus red
  • a pear and freesia scented candle and a reed diffuser
  • a wine opener set
  • a mini gold frame with a print of Caravaggio's Bacchus

Perfect for a birthday, a promotion or a boss who has everything. $69.

The garden-party choice: Sargent The Wineglasses

John Singer Sargent painted The Wineglasses in 1875: two glasses on a table in a sunlit garden, the moment just before friends sit down. It is a painting about the joy of slow evenings together.

The Sargent The Wineglasses Gift Box sets that table:

  • two angled stemless wine glasses with a slanted rim
  • a wooden cheese board with a cheese knife set
  • a sculptural pillar candle and an antique-green bell snuffer
  • a champagne table runner with pearl details
  • roasted almonds in a glass jar
  • a gilded mini frame with a Sargent print

A natural housewarming gift, a thank-you for a host, or an anniversary gift for a couple who love long dinners. $59.
